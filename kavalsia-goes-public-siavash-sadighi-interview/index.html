Five Years Quiet: Siavash Sadighi on Taking Kavalsia Public

Interview by Adele Marchetti

"I didn't need a website... the people who needed to find us already had." Siavash Sadighi says it the way most founders mention the weather. He means it. Kavalsia Inc., his Toronto company, spent five years without a site or a single brand account; if you went looking, you found his LinkedIn and that was it. Now it is going public, and he means something modest by that. A front door, a name on it, a few products shown in daylight. We spoke about the quiet years, the businesses he walked away from, and why he still does not want a sales team. Answers have been condensed.

Part 01

On the private years

Nexus

Five years without a website. Was that a strategy or an accident?

Sadighi

"A bit of both, honestly. At the start I was just busy doing the work... and every hour on a website was an hour I wasn't spending on a client's account. After a while it turned into a choice. If the work's good, it travels on its own."

Nexus

What did private actually look like, day to day?

Sadighi

"No website, no socials for the company... which people find funny given what I do. My LinkedIn was the only door. If you wanted to find me you could... you just had to look."

Nexus

Did staying invisible ever cost you anything?

Sadighi

"Probably. I don't really keep score that way. What it bought me was time to build things properly instead of building a brand around things that weren't finished yet."

Part 02

On the numbers

Nexus

The figure attached to your name is $25M+ in managed ad spend. What does that number mean to you?

Sadighi

"It means I've been trusted with a lot of other people's money, and I learned something from every dollar of it. Most of it's Meta and Google, some programmatic... and I've been at this since 2020, SaaS first, then e-commerce. I'm not precious about it. It's just the job."

Nexus

There were three coaching businesses you scaled to somewhere between $300K and $400K a month. What were they?

Sadighi

"Very different businesses. One was an Amazon selling program, B2C. One was an interior design offer, B2B. One was a gym program. Different buyers, same job for me... build the acquisition system and make it hold."

Nexus

And you left all three.

Sadighi

"I did. The disputes and the chargebacks were just... way too high. Nowhere near what a healthy account should carry. You can grow something like that for a while, but you're really just growing a problem. I'd rather walk away than keep pouring spend into it."

Part 03

On how he works

Nexus

Clients don't get a formal weekly report from you. They get your notes doc, live. Why do it that way?

Sadighi

"Because a report is a summary of what I already know. The notes doc is what I'm actually thinking... the tests, the half-ideas, what broke on Tuesday. Clients can open it whenever they want. Nothing's polished, and that's sort of the point."

Nexus

You work weekends.

Sadighi

"I do. I don't really call it work though... it's lifestyle. Building an acquisition system on a Saturday morning is my idea of a good Saturday."

Nexus

No sales team, no closers, no P&L. You've been clear you want none of it. That's rare at your level.

Sadighi

"Maybe. I know what I love. Building acquisition systems, omni-channel ads, tracking, new funnel structures. Managing closers and owning a P&L is a different job, and somebody else will do it better than me."

Nexus

You still build your own tracking?

Sadighi

"Yeah. Sometimes it's Hyros, sometimes it's hand-coded events. I develop in four languages, so if a tool doesn't do what I need, I'll just write the thing myself."

What it bought me was time to build things properly instead of building a brand around things that weren't finished yet.

Siavash Sadighi
Part 04

On what goes public first

Nexus

What are you actually showing now?

Sadighi

"A few things. Helm is our client operating system... it's live at dashboard.kavalsia.com. It does the onboarding, the first-party tracking, CAPI, offline conversions, attribution across touches, the weekly reports... all in one place."

Nexus

The tracking part is where most readers' eyes glaze over.

Sadighi

"Fair. Short version... the browser fires an event, the server fires the same one, and they carry the same id. So Meta sees one purchase, not two. Then if somebody buys later and it lands in the CRM, Helm ties that sale back to whichever ads got them there. That's the part most setups get wrong."

Nexus

And KidBD?

Sadighi

"That one's personal. It's at kavalsia.com/party... birthday parties for kids. Mom or dad makes a birthday challenge game, and what they get back is a link. That link is the party. No server, no login, nothing sitting in a database. The party lives inside the link... so nothing about anybody's kid is stored anywhere. I really cared about that part."

Nexus

Dalmend?

Sadighi

"Dalmend's a real partnership. They make candles and home fragrance, high end... dark, gold, very considered. We run its acquisition. I won't talk numbers for a partner, but it's exactly the kind of brand I like working with."

Nexus

And the coaching program.

Sadighi

"It's for coaches doing $50k+ a month. Application only, and it's built around the acquisition system. That's honestly all there is to say about it publicly. If it fits, you apply."

Part 05

On influences

Nexus

Which books shaped how you think about advertising?

Sadighi

"Two, mostly. Contagious by Jonah Berger, for why people pass things on. And Breakthrough Advertising by Eugene Schwartz, which I go back to more than I'd like to admit. Schwartz is a big part of why I care so much about where a buyer's head is before they ever see the ad."

Nexus

Is there a tension between a book called Contagious and five years of silence?

Sadighi

"Not really. Being talked about and talking a lot aren't the same thing. I'd rather be the first one."

Part 06

On why now

Nexus

So why go public after five years?

Sadighi

"Because there's finally enough to show. Helm works. KidBD's out. Dalmend's a partner I'm proud of. Five years ago it would've been a website full of promises. Now it's a website about things that exist."

Nexus

What changes for you?

Sadighi

"Honestly, not much. Same weekends, same notes doc. There's just a front door now."

Sadighi ends most answers the way he starts them: flat, unhurried, with the sense that the interesting part is happening somewhere else, probably in a working-notes doc. After five years behind a single LinkedIn profile, Kavalsia Inc. now has the front door he describes, and the programs, Helm and the rest are on the record at kavalsia.com.
