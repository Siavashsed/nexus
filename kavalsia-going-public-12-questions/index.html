Kavalsia Goes Public: 12 Questions, Answered

Going public, in Kavalsia Inc.'s usage, means stepping out from behind a single LinkedIn profile, not listing shares on an exchange. For five years the Toronto acquisition firm kept itself off the open web. Now its name, products and partnerships are going on the record. The twelve questions below are the ones readers ask first. Each answer is short, uses only figures the company states, and stands on its own, so you can jump straight to the number you need from the index.

What is Kavalsia Inc.?

Kavalsia Inc. is a Toronto company that builds and runs acquisition systems for other businesses: paid advertising across Meta, Google Ads and programmatic, the tracking underneath it, and the funnel structures it feeds. Total ad spend under its management: $25M+. Sectors: SaaS and e-commerce, continuously since 2020.

02

Who runs it?

The founder is Siavash Sadighi, known as Sia. Paid acquisition has been his discipline for six-plus years, and he builds each system with his own hands, tracking layer included. He develops in four languages, which is why tracking is sometimes hand-coded rather than bought. Asked about influences, he names Jonah Berger's Contagious first, then Breakthrough Advertising, the Eugene Schwartz classic.

What does going public mean in this case?

It does not mean a stock listing. It means the company now has a public presence: a website and named products and partners, after five years in which it had none of that. In practice that means a reader can now find Kavalsia, its founder and its work in one place, instead of piecing the picture together from a single LinkedIn profile. Nothing about the way the company does its client work is described as changing.

What did the private years look like?

Quiet ones. No site and no company feeds on any platform, for five full years. The only public surface was Sadighi's personal LinkedIn profile. The client work continued throughout; it simply was not marketed through company channels. The $25M+ in managed spend was accumulated during those quiet years.

How much advertising spend has Kavalsia managed?

$25M+ in total, across Meta, Google Ads and programmatic. That is the company's single stated figure. It measures spend managed on behalf of clients, not revenue or profit, and it should not be read as either. It is best understood as the scale of budget clients have trusted the company to allocate, test and optimise since 2020.

06

What is its track record with coaching businesses?

Three of them, all coaching businesses: an Amazon seller training aimed at consumers, interior design work sold to businesses, and, third, a gym program. Each reached $300K-$400K a month under his acquisition. Why he walked away: the dispute and chargeback numbers. Both ran well beyond any healthy account's tolerance. A chargeback is a customer reversing a payment through their bank. High rates put payment processing and ad accounts at risk, which is why the company treats them as a reason to leave rather than a cost of growth.

07

What does Kavalsia build, and what does it decline to do?

Acquisition systems, end to end. That means ads coordinated across channels, tracking in Hyros or in events coded by hand, and funnel structures designed from scratch. It does not build or manage sales teams, does not hire closers and does not take on P&L responsibility. That boundary is deliberate and stated up front. A business that needs a sales floor will need another partner for that part; Kavalsia's work sits in front of the sale, bringing in the buyers and measuring where they came from.

08

How do clients get updates?

No formal weekly report arrives. Each client gets access to the live document where Sadighi keeps his working notes, so tests and the reasoning behind them are visible in real time. Helm, the company's client system, also produces weekly reports. The two do different jobs: the report summarises, the doc shows the working. He works Saturdays and Sundays too; to him that is lifestyle, not overtime. So new entries can appear outside business hours.

09

What is Helm?

Helm is the operating system Kavalsia's clients work inside, and anyone can see it at dashboard.kavalsia.com. Its scope: onboarding new clients; first-party tracking, where a browser hit and its server twin carry the same event id and Meta keeps only one; Meta CAPI; matching offline conversions, so a sale recorded in the CRM is tied back to the ads that led to it; multi-touch attribution; and weekly reports. Deduplication matters because the same purchase is often reported twice, once by the browser and once by the server, and counting it twice overstates results.

10

What is KidBD?

KidBD (kavalsia.com/party) is a paid app for running a child's birthday party. The parent sets up a challenge game around the birthday, and the output is a single shareable link containing every part of the party. Where is the data kept? Nowhere. KidBD runs without a server, asks for no sign-up and keeps no database, because the entire party is encoded in that link, which leaves no stored record about any child.

Who is Dalmend?

Dalmend, a maker of luxury candles and home fragrance, is a genuine Kavalsia partner. Kavalsia runs its acquisition. Kavalsia does not publish sales or return figures for the partnership, consistent with its practice of not sharing a partner's numbers. Dalmend is one of the named relationships made public as part of the company going public.

Is there a program, and where can I read more?

Yes. Coaches whose businesses already bring in $50k+ monthly are who The Coaching Program serves. Entry is by application, and the core of the program is the acquisition system itself. Applications are made at kavalsia.com/coaches. For the current list of programs, see kavalsia.com/Programs.
